**Description**

```
Linear algebra's best free lunch: for a square system, if only zero solves Ax = 0, every b has a solution. Now take an infinite row of boxes and a machine that slides every number one box to the right. Nothing is lost, yet 1, 0, 0, … never comes out. For nineteen episodes every proof used finite dimension. Today five of those facts fall over.

Season 2, episode 20 of Whyme Labs' math series. Part B begins with a ledger of losses. The shift on infinite sequences is one-to-one but not onto, and LS = I while SL ≠ I. Differentiating ε sin(x/ε²) turns a function that flattens onto the axis into one whose slope grows without limit, so there's no biggest stretch. The sequence (1, ½, ⅓, …) has finite energy but no finite sum of basis vectors ever reaches it. In ℓ² the axes e₁, e₂, … are all √2 apart, so the unit ball isn't compact and 1 − 1/k climbs to a maximum it never attains. And multiplication by x has no eigenvectors, only ever narrower tents that come close. Every loss has one cause: Part A was pure algebra, and infinity needs limits.
```
