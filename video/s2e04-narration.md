## Columns are where the basis goes

1. Pick a basis, and write down where a map sends each basis arrow. Those become the columns. That grid of numbers is the matrix.
2. To find where any arrow goes, mix the columns by the arrow's coordinates. The matrix does the map, in numbers.

## Composition forces row × column

1. Now do one map, then another. Where does the first basis arrow end up? Follow it through both.
2. Each entry of the answer is a row of the second matrix against a column of the first. Row times column isn't a convention; following arrows forces it.

## AB ≠ BA

1. Turn a square a quarter turn, then flatten it onto the floor: a short flat shadow. Flatten first, then turn: a tall one.
2. So A times B isn't B times A. Not a strange exception, just socks then shoes.

## Payoff: one map, many shadows

1. Change the basis, and the same map casts a different matrix: a shadow from a new angle. The map hasn't moved.
2. Here a tilted matrix hides a simple map. Turn the light to the diagonals and its shadow is just stretch by 3, stretch by 1.
