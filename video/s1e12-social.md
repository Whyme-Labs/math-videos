## LinkedIn (Whyme Labs page, English)

```
Episode 12 of our math series: the diagonal argument.

It continues the infinite-hotel story from last week. This time a bus arrives with one passenger for every decimal between 0 and 1, and a passenger who walks down the diagonal of the clerk's list finds a name that can't be on it, whatever order the list is in.

That ten-line idea from Cantor in 1891 shows there are more real numbers than whole numbers, and more subsets of any set than members, so infinities come in sizes. The same argument, in different clothes, is behind Russell's paradox and Turing's proof that no program can decide whether every program halts. A consequence: almost every real number can never be named.
```
