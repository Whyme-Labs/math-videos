**Description**

```
Cologne, September 1908. Hermann Minkowski tells a congress of scientists and doctors that space by itself and time by itself will fade away into mere shadows, and only a union of the two survives. That union comes with a strange ruler: it measures the gap between two events with one number, and time counts with a minus sign.

Season 2, episode 9 of Whyme Labs' math series. The grid 2, 1, 1, 2 can be a map (it stretches arrows by 3 and 1) or a bilinear form (it returns 2x² + 2xy + 2y²). Change the basis and the map keeps its stretch factors while the form's grid doesn't: same numbers, different objects. A form is a two-slot machine; fill one slot and a covector is left, so forms change by PᵀBP and maps by P⁻¹AP. Completing squares untangles any symmetric form into plus squares, minus squares and zeros, and Sylvester proved in 1852 that the counts never change: the law of inertia. That count is the signature: ellipses, hyperbolas, parallel lines, and Minkowski's spacetime with signature (+, −, −, −), whose light cone no change of coordinates can remove.
```
