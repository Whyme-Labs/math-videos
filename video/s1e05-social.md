**Single post** (with a 30–60 s clip of the blobs collapsing into a clock):

```
Is 15 o'clock the same as 3 o'clock?

Group every number by its remainder after dividing by 12. Each group becomes one blob, each blob becomes one point, and 12 points make a clock. 15 and 3 land on the same point.

Episode 5: <YouTube link>
```

**Thread** (4 posts):

1. `Is 1/2 the same as 2/4? As amounts, yes. As pairs of numbers, (1, 2) and (2, 4) are different. Math deals with this by declaring things the same, and there's a right way to do it. 🧵`
2. `A rule for "the same" needs three properties: everything matches itself, matching goes both ways, and matches chain. "Differs by less than 1" fails the last one: 0 ~ 0.6 ~ 1.2, but 0 and 1.2 differ by 1.2.`
3. `With a good rule, things fall into blobs that never partly overlap. Then you check every operation. On a clock, + is fine: 9 + 5 and 21 + 17 land in the same blob. 2ⁿ isn't: 0 and 12 share a blob, but 2⁰ = 1 and 2¹² = 4096 leave different remainders.`
4. `Fractions are built the same way: one half is the whole blob {(1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6), …}. Allow (0, 0) and it matches every pair, so every fraction glues into one blob. That's why the bottom can't be 0. Episode 5: <YouTube link>`
