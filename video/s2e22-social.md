**Description**

```
Pick a number between 0 and 1 at random. What's the chance it's a fraction? Fractions are everywhere, yet the chance is zero. Stranger: delete the middle third of [0, 1], then the middle third of each piece, forever. You remove total length 1, yet as many points as ℝ remain. Counting and measuring are different questions.

Season 2, episode 22 of Whyme Labs' math series. Riemann integrates by cutting the x-axis into strips, and Dirichlet's function, 1 on fractions and 0 elsewhere, breaks it: every strip has upper sum 1 and lower sum 0, even though the function is a limit of functions with integral 0. So what should size mean? Intervals keep their length, sliding doesn't change size, and countably many separate pieces add. The fractions get covers of length ε/2, ε/4, ε/8, …, so their size is zero. The Cantor set has length zero, but its base-3 digits 0 and 2 turn into every binary number. Vitali's set shows some sets can't be given a size at all. Lebesgue slices the heights instead of the x-axis, which gives ⅓ for x², 0 for Dirichlet's function, and lets limits and integrals swap. Functions equal almost everywhere are one element of L¹, and probability, after Kolmogorov, is measure.
```
