## The swap behind the quadratic formula

1. Take a quadratic with roots r one and r two. Swap them: their sum and their product don't change, and those are the coefficients you're given.
2. Their difference flips sign when you swap. Its square doesn't, so the square is known, and one square root brings back the difference. That's the quadratic formula.

## The group of an equation

1. Galois's idea: for any equation, collect every way its roots can trade places without breaking a single rational relation among them. That's its group.
2. A formula climbs down a ladder, one root sign per rung, and each rung must be a step where the moves commute. For degrees 2, 3 and 4, the ladder reaches the bottom.

## Payoff: five roots, and the ladder jams

1. Five roots, and the ladder jams. With five to shuffle, every three-cycle is a commutator of two others, so no rung can ever get rid of them.
2. x to the fifth minus 4x plus 2 has all 120 shuffles as its group. The ladder never reaches the bottom, so no formula of roots can reach its roots.
