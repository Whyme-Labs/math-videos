**Description**

```
Take a narrow tent at 0 and keep making it narrower. Its area goes to 0, so by area the tents are shrinking to nothing. But the value at 0 reads 1, every time. That measurement is linear and it can't be trusted, like a thermometer that reads 100° on ice. In infinite dimensions we keep only the measurements that are continuous.

Season 2, episode 24 of Whyme Labs' math series. In finite dimensions every linear functional is continuous. In infinite dimensions monsters appear, like f(x) = Σ k·xₖ, which sends each unit vector eₙ to n. The continuous dual X* keeps only the bounded functionals; a functional's norm is its top reading on the unit ball, and X* is always complete because ℝ is. Hölder's inequality shows every y in ℓʳ measures ℓᵖ when 1/p + 1/r = 1, and the dual unit balls pair up: diamond with square, disc with itself. In fact (ℓᵖ)* = ℓʳ for 1 ≤ p < ∞, because finite sequences are dense; in ℓ∞ they aren't, so (ℓ∞)* is strictly bigger than ℓ¹. The dual of C[0, 1] is the measures on [0, 1] (Riesz). Point evaluation is continuous for the peak norm but not for the area norm. Last, the double dual X** and reflexivity: yes for ℓᵖ with 1 < p < ∞, no for ℓ¹ and C[0, 1].
```
