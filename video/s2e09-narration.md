1. Cologne, September 1908. Hermann Minkowski stands before a congress of scientists and doctors and announces the end of space and time.

## One grid, two objects

1. Here's a grid: 2, 1, 1, 2. As a map, it stretches arrows. As a form, it takes an arrow twice and returns 2x squared plus 2xy plus 2y squared.
2. Change the basis. The map's new grid keeps its stretch factors, 3 and 1. The form's new grid doesn't. Same numbers, different objects.

## A form is a map V → V*

1. A bilinear form is a machine with two slots, linear in each. Fill one slot and what's left is a covector: a stack of sheets.
2. So a form turns vectors into covectors. That's why its grid changes by P transpose B P, while a map's changes by P inverse A P.

## Completing squares, Sylvester's law

1. Any symmetric form can be untangled by completing squares: some plus squares, some minus squares, maybe some zeros.
2. In 1852 Sylvester proved the counts never change, however you untangle it. He called it the law of inertia.

## Payoff: signatures and Minkowski's spacetime

1. So every form has a signature. Plus, plus draws ellipses. Plus, minus draws hyperbolas. Plus and a zero draws parallel lines.
2. Minkowski's spacetime is plus, minus, minus, minus. No change of coordinates turns it into ordinary distance: the light cone is that signature.
