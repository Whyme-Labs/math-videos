## Rules for a signed area

1. Ask for a signed area of two arrows. Stretch one arrow and the area stretches. Slide one arrow along the other and nothing changes.
2. Swap the arrows and the sign flips. Make them parallel and the area is zero. Those rules fix the area completely, once you pick a unit.

## The wedge

1. Call it the wedge, v wedge w: an oriented patch of area. In three dimensions, wedges of two arrows fill three directions, and wedges of three fill just one.
2. In n dimensions the top wedge always has one direction. So a linear map can only do one thing to it: multiply it by a single number.

## The determinant is volume

1. That number is the determinant. Push the unit cube through the map and its new volume is the determinant. Turn it inside out and the sign goes negative.
2. Crush the cube flat and the determinant is zero: the map has lost a direction, and nothing can undo it.

## Payoff: det(AB), and the formula

1. Now the old miracle. Scale volumes by 2, then by 3, and you've scaled them by 6. So the determinant of AB is det A times det B.
2. And the six-term formula? Expand the wedge of the three columns, and the terms fall out by themselves, signs and all.
