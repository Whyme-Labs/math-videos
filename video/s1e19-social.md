## LinkedIn (Whyme Labs page, English)

```
Episode 19 of our math series: fields.

For two thousand years people tried to trisect an angle with a straightedge and compass. In 1837 Pierre Wantzel showed it can't be done, and the tool he needed was linear algebra in disguise.

A field is any setting where you can add, subtract, multiply and divide. Each compass step extends the field by at most a square root, which at most doubles its dimension (its "degree"). Degrees multiply along a chain of extensions, so anything constructible has degree a power of 2. The cosine of 20° has degree 3. That one number settles both trisection and doubling the cube.

The Chinese edition adds a look at 规矩, the compass and square of classical China, from Mencius and the Tang silk paintings of Fuxi and Nüwa.
```
