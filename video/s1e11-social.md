**Thread** (4 posts):

1. `Are there fewer even numbers than whole numbers? 🧵`
2. `They look like half. But pair them up: 0↔0, 1↔2, 2↔4, n↔2n. Every number gets exactly one partner, nobody is left over. By the only definition of "same size" that works for infinite sets, they're equal.`
3. `It goes further. Infinitely many buses, each with infinitely many passengers? Walk the car park in diagonals, each one finite, and everybody gets a room.`
4. `Even the fractions fit. Grow them on the Calkin–Wilf tree: a/b has children a/(a+b) and (a+b)/b. Every positive fraction appears once, in lowest terms. Read it row by row. Episode 11: <YouTube link>`

It opens with a story. At midnight a traveler reaches a hotel with infinitely many rooms, every one taken, and the clerk still finds a room by asking every guest to move one door along.
