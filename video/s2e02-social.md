**Description**

```
London, May 1861. James Clerk Maxwell stands in the Royal Institution with three magic lanterns. A tartan ribbon was photographed through red, green and blue filters; the three lanterns throw the pictures onto one screen, and the ribbon appears in colour. Three lights were enough. Why three, and not two, or ten?

Season 2, episode 2 of Whyme Labs' math series. Mix vectors in any amounts and you get their span; an arrow you could already have made is redundant. A basis makes everything with nothing wasted, and Steinitz's exchange shows that every basis of a space has the same size: swap one basis into the other a vector at a time, and the count never changes. That count is the dimension, and it belongs to the space. Coordinates don't: change the basis and the same arrow gets new numbers. Then the spaces that never run out: polynomials need 1, x, x², … forever, and the reals over the rationals need uncountably many ingredients, a basis nobody has ever written down.
```
