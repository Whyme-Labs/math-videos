## Isomorphism

1. Two groups are the same when you can relabel one as the other and every move still matches: quarter turn, one hour, times i.
2. That relabeling is an isomorphism. Same size isn't enough. A rectangle also has four moves, but each one undoes itself in two steps. The clock's one hour needs four.

## A map that forgets: the kernel

1. Now a map that forgets. Send every integer to its hour on the four-hour clock. Add, then convert, or convert, then add: you land in the same place.
2. A map that respects the moves like that is a homomorphism. Everything that lands on 0, the multiples of 4, is its kernel: exactly what the map forgets.

## Cosets and Lagrange

1. The kernel slices the integers into four lanes: the multiples of 4, then one more, two more, three more. Each lane is a coset.
2. In a finite group, the slices are all the same size, so a subgroup's size always divides the group's. That's Lagrange's theorem.

## The first isomorphism theorem

1. Squash each lane to a single point, and the four points form a group of their own: the four-hour clock.
2. That's the first isomorphism theorem. Every homomorphism is a collapse, followed by a relabeling.
