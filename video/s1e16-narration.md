## Cold open: what did your friend do?

1. Close your eyes. Your friend picks up a square tile, does something to it, and puts it back exactly in its outline.
2. Open them. Nothing looks different. Did they turn it a quarter, a half, three quarters, or not at all?
3. Or flip it, across either diagonal or either middle line? That's eight moves, and you can't tell them apart.
4. The same game works on a clock: add five hours. And on a Rubik's cube, with about 43 quintillion scrambles.
5. Three different worlds, and their moves follow the same few rules. What are they?

## Title

1. Season one, episode sixteen. Groups.

## The four rules

1. Rule one: a move followed by a move is a move. Rule two: doing nothing counts as a move.
2. Rule three: every move can be undone. Rule four: in a long chain of moves, it doesn't matter how you group them.
3. Any collection of moves with these four rules is called a group. Nothing says what the moves are made of, only what they do.

## What fails the rules

1. Whole numbers with adding fail rule three: you can't undo add three without going below zero. That's why episode six built the integers.
2. Subtraction fails rule four: 5 minus 3, then minus 1, is 1. But 5 minus the result of 3 minus 1 is 3.

## Order matters: socks and shoes

1. On the square, order matters. Flip then turn, and corner 1 lands where 2 was. Turn then flip, and it lands where 4 was.
2. So undo in reverse. You put on socks, then shoes. To undo that, shoes come off first.

## Counting is one move

1. And counting? Counting is one move, done again and again. Three is: do it three times. Minus three: undo it three times.
2. The integers are what one undoable move builds when nothing else is assumed. On a clock, twelve steps bring you home. In the integers, you never come back.
