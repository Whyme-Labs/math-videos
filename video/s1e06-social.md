## X (x.com), English

**Single post** (with a 30–60 s clip of the diagonals collapsing onto the number line):

```
What is 3 − 5 when only natural numbers exist?

Write it as the pair (3, 5). Pairs like (0, 2), (1, 3), (2, 4) "should" give the same answer, and they all sit on one diagonal of a grid. That whole diagonal is −2.

Episode 6: <YouTube link>
```

**Thread** (4 posts):

1. `Why is minus times minus plus? It isn't a convention someone picked. Any number system with negatives and the distributive law is forced into it. 🧵`
2. `Build the integers from pairs of natural numbers: (a, b) stands for a − b, and (a, b) ~ (c, d) when a + d = b + c. The multiplication formula [a, b]·[c, d] = [ac + bd, ad + bc] gives (−1)·(−1) = [0, 1]·[0, 1] = [1, 0] = 1.`
3. `Or skip the formula: 0 = (−1)·0 = (−1)·(1 + (−1)) = −1 + (−1)·(−1). Add 1 to both sides. The distributive law leaves no other choice.`
4. `Fractions come from pairs the same way, and there's a trap: "add tops, add bottoms" gives 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5, but 2/4 + 1/3 = 3/7. Same numbers, two answers, so it isn't an operation on numbers. Episode 6: <YouTube link>`

With only natural numbers, 3 − 5 has no answer. The usual fix was to invent a symbol, −2, plus rules to memorise. Fractions follow the same pattern, as lines through the centre of a grid.
