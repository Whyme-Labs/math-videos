**Description**

```
Göttingen, 1908. Erhard Schmidt, a student of Hilbert, treats infinite lists of numbers with Euclid's words: length, angle, right angle. Try that on functions. Are 1 and x perpendicular? By their coefficients, yes. By their overlap from −1 to 1, yes. By their overlap from 0 to 1, no: they meet at exactly 30°. Three honest answers that disagree.

Season 2, episode 14 of Whyme Labs' math series. Dotting coordinates can't be a ruler: change the basis and two fixed arrows go from 'perpendicular' to anything at all. So length and angle have to be chosen: an inner product, a rule that is linear, symmetric and positive, each rule acted out on screen. Positive is the one that matters: drawn as heights, an inner product is a bowl, while a saddle like x² − y² dips below zero. Slice the bowl at height 1 and you get its unit circle, an ellipse; stretch the plane until it is round and every inner product turns out to be the dot product in disguise. Then a real proof of Cauchy–Schwarz: slide u − tv along v and its squared length traces a parabola that never goes below zero. That gives angles, and the cold open's 30° is computed from three areas. The payoff: taxicab and chessboard lengths fail Pythagoras, and the parallelogram law (Jordan–von Neumann, 1935) balances only for the round ruler.
```
