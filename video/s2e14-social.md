**Description**

```
Göttingen, 1908. Erhard Schmidt, a student of Hilbert, treats infinite lists of numbers with Euclid's words: length, angle, right angle. Try that on functions. Are 1 and x perpendicular? By their coefficients, yes. By their overlap from −1 to 1, yes. By their overlap from 0 to 1, no: they meet at exactly 30°. Three honest answers that disagree.

Season 2, episode 14 of Whyme Labs' math series. A vector space only knows how to add and scale. Multiplying coordinates doesn't give a ruler, because switching basis changes the answer while nothing on screen moves. So length and angle have to be chosen: an inner product, a symmetric form that gives every nonzero arrow a positive squared length. Each choice has its own unit circle (an ellipse) and its own right angles. Whatever you choose, Cauchy–Schwarz holds: overlap never beats length × length, so the cosine of an angle always lands between −1 and 1. The payoff: some rulers measure length with no angles at all. The taxi ruler's unit circle is a diamond, the max ruler's a square, and in 1935 Jordan and von Neumann showed the parallelogram law picks out exactly the rulers that come from an inner product. Only p = 2 balances.
```
