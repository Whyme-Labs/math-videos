## LinkedIn (Whyme Labs page, English)

```
Episode 9 of our math series: why 0.999… = 1.

It might be the most argued-about equation online, and the skeptics have a fair point: nobody can write infinitely many nines, so what does 0.999… even name? The episode answers that before proving anything. A decimal names the least upper bound of its finite truncations, and that bound exists because the real numbers are complete.

From there the proof is short. If the bound sat below 1, the gap would be smaller than every power of one tenth, an infinitesimal, and the Archimedean property rules those out in ℝ. The episode also shows which numbers have two decimal names, and why the popular 10x − x trick is a shortcut rather than a proof.

The Chinese edition adds a line from the Zhuangzi: take half of a one-foot stick every day and it never runs out. In binary, the halves taken add up to exactly the whole stick: 0.111… = 1.
```
