## Span, and a redundant arrow

1. Mix vectors in any amounts and you get their span. Two arrows that point different ways span the whole plane.
2. Add a third arrow and you gain nothing: it was already a mix of the first two. An ingredient you could have made is redundant.

## The exchange: every basis the same size

1. A basis is a set of ingredients that makes everything, with nothing redundant. Could one basis have three vectors and another four?
2. No. Swap the vectors of one basis into the other, one at a time. Each one evicts a vector and takes its seat, and the list still makes everything. So the counts match.

## Coordinates are a language

1. That count is the dimension, and it belongs to the space. The coordinates don't: change the basis and the same arrow gets new numbers.
2. A basis is a language. The vector is the thing; its coordinates are how one language spells it.

## Infinite dimension

1. Some spaces never run out. Polynomials need 1, x, x squared, and on forever: no finite list makes them all.
2. And the real numbers, scaled only by fractions, need uncountably many ingredients. A basis exists only by the axiom of choice, and nobody has ever written one down.
