**Description**

```
Paris, 1795. A new law makes the gram the unit of weight, and a grocer has to rewrite every number in the shop. A basket of 2 kg of apples, 1 kg of flour and half a kilo of sugar becomes 2000, 1000 and 500 grams: the numbers grow a thousandfold. The prices per gram shrink a thousandfold. The bill doesn't move: 10.50 francs. Two lists of three numbers that change in opposite ways can't be the same kind of thing.

Season 2, episode 6 of Whyme Labs' math series. The basket is a vector; the price list is a covector, a linear machine that eats a vector and returns a number. Draw it as a stack of evenly spaced sheets: its reading is how many sheets an arrow crosses. Doubling an arrow makes it longer; doubling a covector packs its sheets twice as close, which is the grocer's puzzle drawn. A basis comes with a dual basis of coordinate readers, and the first reader depends on the second arrow too. The payoff: zoom in on a contour map until the contours turn straight and evenly spaced. That stack is df, and the gradient was a covector all along; the familiar gradient arrow only appears once you decide what "perpendicular" means.
```
