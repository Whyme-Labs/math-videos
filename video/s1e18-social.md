**Description**

```
Paris, March 1847. Gabriel Lamé tells the Academy of Sciences he has proved Fermat's Last Theorem, by letting new numbers into arithmetic and comparing prime factors. Joseph Liouville asks the fatal question: do these numbers split into primes in only one way? That May, a letter from Ernst Kummer answers: no.

Episode 18 of "What Is a Number?". A ring is any world where you can add, subtract and multiply, with multiplying spreading over adding, and that one rule forces (−1)(−1) = 1. On a six-hour clock 2 × 3 = 0; on a seven-hour clock every nonzero number has a partner. Glue the rule x² + 1 = 0 into polynomials and you get exactly the complex numbers. And in the numbers a + b√−5, 6 = 2 × 3 = (1 + √−5)(1 − √−5): two factorizations into pieces that can't be split. Kummer and Dedekind's repair, ideals, makes factorization unique again.
```
